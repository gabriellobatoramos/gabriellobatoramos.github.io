Working Papers

The Local Cost of Partisanship

with Chun Zhao

Abstract

Whether partisanship reaches into local economies, and through what mechanism, remains an open question. We provide an answer using the U.S. municipal bond market. Combining a close-gubernatorial-election design that delivers quasi-random variation in the partisan alignment between state and federal executives with the yields at which local governments borrow, we establish three findings. First, municipal bonds issued in misaligned states carry materially higher yields. Second, the increase is not driven by changes in the local tax base or by investor pessimism; tax revenues and revenue-bond yields are unaffected. It reflects a contraction in federal intergovernmental transfers, concentrated in the discretionary categories the federal executive controls. Third, the effect reaches the real economy: misaligned counties lose private-sector employment and personal income.

Polarizing Corporations: Does Talent Flow to “Good” Firms?

with Emanuele Colonnelli, Tim McQuade, Thomas Rauter, and Olivia Xiong

Revise & Resubmit · Journal of Political Economy

Abstract

We conduct a field experiment in partnership with the largest job platform in Brazil to study how environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices of firms affect talent allocation. We find both an average job-seeker’s preference for ESG and a large degree of heterogeneity across sociodemographic groups, with the strongest preference displayed by highly educated, white, and politically liberal individuals. Combining the experimental estimates with matched employer-employee administrative microdata, we estimate an equilibrium model of the labor market in which firms endogenously adopt ESG. Counterfactual analyses show that ESG preferences draw skilled workers toward high-productivity firms, increasing allocative efficiency, total output, and worker welfare.
